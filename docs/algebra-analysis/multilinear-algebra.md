# Multilinear Algebra

This chapter builds the three fundamental graded algebras attached to a module
$V$ over a commutative ring $K$: the **tensor algebra** $T(V)$, the
**symmetric algebra** $S(V)$ and the **exterior algebra** $\Lambda(V)$. The
exterior algebra yields the theory of **determinants** of endomorphisms of a
free module and a clean criterion for linear independence in terms of wedge
products.

## Tensor Product

!!! theorem "Theorem (Tensor product via representability)"
    <a id="thm-ml-1-1"></a>
    Let $K$ be a commutative ring, $V_1, \ldots, V_n$ be $K$-modules. The
    functor $W \mapsto \mathrm{Hom}_K^{(n)}(V_1, \ldots, V_n; W)$ from
    $\mathrm{Mod}_K$ to $\mathrm{Set}$ is representable. The representing
    object is the **tensor product**

    $$
    V_1 \otimes_K V_2 \otimes_K \cdots \otimes_K V_n
    = K^{\oplus (V_1 \times \cdots \times V_n)} / M,
    $$

    where $M$ is the sub-module generated by all
    $e_{(x_1, \ldots, ax_i' + bx_i'', \ldots, x_n)} - a\, e_{(\ldots, x_i', \ldots)}
    - b\, e_{(\ldots, x_i'', \ldots)}$.

??? proof "Proof (Tensor product via representability)"
    Let $(e_x)_{x \in V_1 \times \cdots \times V_n}$ be the canonical
    basis of $F = K^{\oplus (V_1 \times \cdots \times V_n)}$. For any
    $K$-module $W$ and any function $s : V_1 \times \cdots \times V_n \to W$,
    define $f_s : F \to W$ by $f_s(e_x) = s(x)$. The assignment
    $s \mapsto f_s$ is a bijection from $W^{V_1 \times \cdots \times V_n}$
    to $\mathrm{Hom}_K(F, W)$, functorial in $W$.
    For $s$ to be $n$-linear, $f_s$ must vanish on the sub-module $M$
    generated by the relations in the statement. Conversely, if $f_s$
    vanishes on $M$, then $f_s$ descends to a $K$-linear map
    $\bar f_s : F/M \to W$, and $s$ is $n$-linear iff $\bar f_s(e_x) =
    s(x)$ forces the linearity conditions on $s$. So
    $W \mapsto \mathrm{Hom}_K^{(n)}(V_1, \ldots, V_n; W)$ is in natural
    bijection with $W \mapsto \mathrm{Hom}_K(F/M, W)$, i.e. the tensor
    product $F/M$ represents the $n$-linearity functor.

!!! definition "Definition (Pure tensor)"
    <a id="def-ml-1-2"></a>
    For $(x_1, \ldots, x_n) \in V_1 \times \cdots \times V_n$, the class
    of the basis vector $e_{(x_1, \ldots, x_n)}$ in $V_1 \otimes \cdots
    \otimes V_n$ is the **pure tensor** $x_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes x_n$.
    The universal property: every $n$-linear
    $f : V_1 \times \cdots \times V_n \to W$ factors uniquely through
    $V_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes V_n$.

!!! proposition "Proposition (Associativity)"
    <a id="prop-ml-1-3"></a>
    For any partition of $\{1, \ldots, n\}$ into consecutive blocks,
    the iterated tensor product is canonically isomorphic to the full
    tensor product via the map
    $x_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes x_n \mapsto x_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes
    (x_i \otimes \cdots \otimes x_{i+j}) \otimes \cdots \otimes x_n$.

??? proof "Proof (Associativity)"
    Define the map
    $\iota : V_1 \times \cdots \times V_n \to V_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes V_{i-1}
    \otimes (V_i \otimes \cdots \otimes V_{i+j}) \otimes V_{i+j+1} \otimes
    \cdots \otimes V_n$ by sending $(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$ to
    $x_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes (x_i \otimes \cdots \otimes x_{i+j}) \otimes
    \cdots \otimes x_n$. This map is $n$-linear (it is $K$-linear in
    each slot separately), so by the universal property of the tensor
    product it induces a $K$-linear map
    $\bar\iota : V_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes V_n \to V_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes
    (V_i \otimes \cdots \otimes V_{i+j}) \otimes \cdots \otimes V_n$.

    To show $\bar\iota$ is an isomorphism, observe that both
    $V_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes V_n$ and the iterated product represent
    the same functor $W \mapsto \mathrm{Hom}_K^{(n)}(V_1, \ldots, V_n;
    W)$ (an $n$-linear map factors uniquely through either tensor
    product). Indeed, the iterated $n$-linear hom-space
    $\mathrm{Hom}_K^{(n)}(V_1, \ldots, V_{i-1}, (V_i \otimes \cdots
    \otimes V_{i+j}), V_{i+j+1}, \ldots, V_n; W)$ is the same as
    $\mathrm{Hom}_K^{(n)}(V_1, \ldots, V_n; W)$ by the universal property
    of the inner tensor product. By the Yoneda lemma (Thm 1.4.1),
    both tensor products are isomorphic to the representing object, so
    they are isomorphic to each other.

## Tensor Algebra

!!! definition "Definition (Tensor algebra)"
    <a id="def-ml-2-1"></a>
    Let $V$ be a $K$-module and $V^{\otimes n} = V \otimes_K \cdots
    \otimes_K V$ ($n$ copies, $V^{\otimes 0} = K$). The **tensor
    algebra** of $V$ is the $K$-algebra

    $$
    T(V) = \bigoplus_{n \in \mathbb{N}} V^{\otimes n},
    $$

    with multiplication induced by the concatenation of pure tensors:
    $(x_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes x_n) \cdot (y_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes
    y_m) = x_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes x_n \otimes y_1 \otimes \cdots
    \otimes y_m$. It is a **graded $K$-algebra**.

!!! theorem "Theorem (Universal property of $T(V)$)"
    <a id="thm-ml-2-2"></a>
    The functor $A \mapsto \mathrm{Hom}_K(V, A)$ from $\mathrm{Alg}_K$ to
    $\mathrm{Set}$ is represented by $T(V)$. In particular, every
    $K$-linear map $f : V \to A$ extends uniquely to a $K$-algebra
    homomorphism $\lambda_f : T(V) \to A$ with
    $\lambda_f(x_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes x_n) = f(x_1) \cdots f(x_n)$.

??? proof "Proof (Universal property of $T(V)$)"
    **Uniqueness.** $T(V)$ is generated as a $K$-algebra by $V$ (embedded
    via $v \mapsto v$). So any $K$-algebra map $\lambda_f : T(V) \to A$
    extending $f$ is determined by
    $\lambda_f(v_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes v_n) = \lambda_f(v_1) \cdots
    \lambda_f(v_n) = f(v_1) \cdots f(v_n)$.
    **Existence.** Define the $n$-linear map
    $V^n \to A$, $(v_1, \ldots, v_n) \mapsto f(v_1) \cdots f(v_n)$.
    By the universal property of the tensor product this induces a
    $K$-linear map $\lambda_f^{(n)} : V^{\otimes n} \to A$ with
    $\lambda_f^{(n)}(v_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes v_n) = f(v_1) \cdots f(v_n)$.
    The family $(\lambda_f^{(n)})_n$ assembles into a $K$-linear map
    $\lambda_f : T(V) = \bigoplus V^{\otimes n} \to A$, which is
    multiplicative by
    $\lambda_f(u \otimes v) = f(u_1) \cdots f(u_p) f(v_1) \cdots f(v_q)
    = \lambda_f(u) \lambda_f(v)$ for pure tensors $u \in V^{\otimes p}$,
    $v \in V^{\otimes q}$, and extends by linearity.

!!! corollary "Corollary (Functoriality of $T$)"
    <a id="cor-ml-2-3"></a>
    A $K$-linear map $f : V \to W$ induces a $K$-algebra homomorphism
    $T(f) : T(V) \to T(W)$ with
    $T(f)(x_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes x_n) = f(x_1) \otimes \cdots \otimes
    f(x_n)$.

??? proof "Proof (Functoriality of $T$)"
    Apply Thm 4.2.2 with the target algebra $A = T(W)$ and the
    $K$-linear map $V \to T(W)$, $v \mapsto f(v)$ (well defined since
    $f$ is $K$-linear). The image of $x_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes x_n$
    is $f(x_1) \cdots f(x_n) = f(x_1) \otimes \cdots \otimes f(x_n)$ in
    $T(W)$, so the resulting $K$-algebra map $T(V) \to T(W)$ has the
    stated form.

!!! definition "Definition (Graded algebra, homogeneous ideal)"
    <a id="def-ml-2-4"></a>
    A **graded $K$-algebra** is a $K$-algebra $A = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0}
    A_n$ with $A_n \cdot A_m \subseteq A_{n+m}$. An ideal $I \subseteq A$
    is **homogeneous** if it is generated by homogeneous elements; then
    $I = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} I \cap A_n$ and $A / I$ inherits a
    grading.

## Symmetric Algebra

??? proof "Proof (Universal property of $S(V)$)"
    Since every permutation is a product of transpositions
    (Thm 9-6-9 on $S_n$), and
    $x \otimes y - y \otimes x \in I$ generates a homogeneous ideal
    stable under adjacent transpositions, the relation
    $x_{\sigma(1)} \otimes \cdots \otimes x_{\sigma(n)} - x_1 \otimes \cdots
    \otimes x_n \in I$ holds for every $\sigma \in S_n$. Hence $S(V)$ is
    commutative.
    For any commutative $A$ and $K$-linear $f : V \to A$, the
    $K$-algebra map $\lambda_f : T(V) \to A$ (Thm 9-3-1 from chapter 9)
    satisfies $\lambda_f(x \otimes y - y \otimes x) = f(x) f(y) - f(y)
    f(x) = 0$ by commutativity of $A$. So $\lambda_f$ vanishes on $I$
    and descends to a $K$-algebra map $S(V) \to A$ extending $f$.
    Uniqueness: $S(V)$ is generated as a $K$-algebra by the image of $V$,
    so any such map is determined by its restriction to $V$.

!!! definition "Definition (Symmetric algebra, polynomial algebra)"
    <a id="def-ml-3-2"></a>
    The **symmetric algebra** $S(V) = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} S^n(V)$ is a
    graded commutative $K$-algebra with $S^0(V) = K$ and $S^1(V) = V$.
    For $V$ a free module with basis $(T_1, \ldots, T_d)$, $S(V) =
    K[T_1, \ldots, T_d]$ is the **polynomial algebra** in $d$ variables.

## Exterior Algebra

!!! definition "Definition (Exterior algebra, wedge product)"
    <a id="def-ml-4-1"></a>
    The **exterior algebra** of a $K$-module $V$ is the quotient
    $K$-algebra

    $$
    \Lambda(V) = T(V) / J,\qquad
    J = \langle\, x \otimes x : x \in V \,\rangle.
    $$

    Its homogeneous component of degree $n$ is $\Lambda^n(V)$, and the
    product of $\Lambda(V)$ is the **wedge product** $\wedge$. For
    $(x_1, \ldots, x_n) \in V^n$, $x_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge x_n$ is the
    class of $x_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes x_n$.

!!! definition "Definition (Sign of a permutation)"
    <a id="def-ml-4-2"></a>
    The **sign homomorphism** $\mathrm{sgn} : S_n \to \{\pm 1\}$ sends
    every transposition to $-1$. Explicitly,

    $$
    \mathrm{sgn}(\sigma) = \prod_{1 \leq i < j \leq n} \frac{\sigma(j) -
    \sigma(i)}{j - i}.
    $$

    The sign is multiplicative, well-defined (every $\sigma$ is a
    product of transpositions, with sign $(-1)^{\text{number}}$), and
    depends only on the cycle structure of $\sigma$.

!!! proposition "Proposition (Anticommutativity)"
    <a id="prop-ml-4-3"></a>
    For any $(x_1, \ldots, x_n) \in V^n$ and $\sigma \in S_n$,

    $$
    x_{\sigma(1)} \wedge \cdots \wedge x_{\sigma(n)} = \mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\,
    x_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge x_n.
    $$

    In particular $x \wedge y = - y \wedge x$ and $x \wedge x = 0$.

??? proof "Proof (Anticommutativity)"
    For $n = 2$, expand $(x + y) \wedge (x + y) = x \wedge x +
    x \wedge y + y \wedge x + y \wedge y = x \wedge y + y \wedge x$
    (using $x \wedge x = 0$ and $y \wedge y = 0$), so
    $0 = (x + y) \wedge (x + y) = x \wedge y + y \wedge x$, i.e.
    $y \wedge x = -x \wedge y$.
    For general $\sigma$, by Thm 9-6-9 every $\sigma \in S_n$ is a
    product of transpositions $(i\; j)$. By the $n = 2$ case,
    each transposition contributes a factor $-1$ (the remaining
    variables are unchanged), so the result is $\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)
    \cdot x_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge x_n$. Explicitly, it suffices to
    check on adjacent transpositions $(i\; i+1)$:
    $x_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge x_{i+1} \wedge x_i \wedge \cdots \wedge
    x_n = -x_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge x_n$ (swap the two
    consecutive slots and use $x \wedge y = -y \wedge x$).

!!! proposition "Proposition (Universal property of $\Lambda^n$)"
    <a id="prop-ml-4-4"></a>
    Every alternating $n$-linear $f : V^n \to M$ factors uniquely
    through a $K$-linear $\hat f : \Lambda^n(V) \to M$ with
    $\hat f(x_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge x_n) = f(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$.

??? proof "Proof (Universal property of $\Lambda^n$)"
    **Uniqueness.** The pure tensors $x_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge x_n$ span
    $\Lambda^n(V)$ (by definition), so any $K$-linear $\hat f$ on
    $\Lambda^n(V)$ is determined by its values on pure tensors, which
    are forced by the relation.
    **Existence.** By the universal property of the tensor product
    (Thm 4.1.1), $f$ induces a $K$-linear map $\tilde f : V^{\otimes n}
    \to M$ with $\tilde f(x_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes x_n) = f(x_1, \ldots,
    x_n)$. Since $f$ is alternating, $\tilde f$ vanishes on every
    element of the form $x_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes x_n$ with two
    equal slots (and hence on the sub-module generated by such
    elements, which is precisely the kernel of $V^{\otimes n} \to
    \Lambda^n(V)$). So $\tilde f$ descends to a $K$-linear map
    $\hat f : \Lambda^n(V) \to M$ with $\hat f(x_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge
    x_n) = f(x_1, \ldots, x_n)$.

    **Consequence.** If $f(x_1, \ldots, x_n) \neq 0$ for some
    alternating $n$-linear $f$, then $\hat f(x_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge
    x_n) = f(x_1, \ldots, x_n) \neq 0$, forcing $x_1 \wedge \cdots
    \wedge x_n \neq 0$.

!!! example "Example (Wedge of linear forms)"
    <a id="ex-ml-4-5"></a>
    Given $n$ linear forms $f_1, \ldots, f_n$ on $V$, the formula

    $$
    (f_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge f_n)(x_1, \ldots, x_n) = \sum_{\sigma \in S_n}
    \mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\, f_{\sigma(1)}(x_1) \cdots f_{\sigma(n)}(x_n)
    $$

    gives an alternating $n$-linear form, hence a linear form
    $f_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge f_n \in \Lambda^n(V)^\vee$. With $(e_i)$
    a basis of $V$ and $(e_i^\vee)$ its dual,
    $(e_1^\vee \wedge \cdots \wedge e_n^\vee)(e_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge e_n) =
    1$.

## Linear Independence Criterion

!!! lemma "Lemma (Wedge and linear combinations)"
    <a id="lem-ml-5-1"></a>
    If $x_i = \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{i, j} e_j$ in a $K$-module $V$, then

    $$
    x_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge x_n = \left( \sum_{\sigma \in S_n}
    \mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\, a_{1, \sigma(1)} \cdots a_{n, \sigma(n)} \right)
    e_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge e_n.
    $$

??? proof "Proof (Wedge and linear combinations)"
    By multilinearity of the wedge, expand
    $x_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge x_n = \sum_{j_1, \ldots, j_n}
    a_{1, j_1} \cdots a_{n, j_n} e_{j_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge e_{j_n}$.
    The terms with two equal indices (some $j_i = j_k$) vanish
    because $e_j \wedge e_j = 0$. So only $n$-tuples with all $j_i$
    distinct contribute. For a permutation $\sigma$, the term
    $e_{j_{\sigma(1)}} \wedge \cdots \wedge e_{j_{\sigma(n)}}$ is
    equal to $\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma) e_{j_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge
    e_{j_n}$ by Prop 4-4-3. Set $j_i = \sigma(i)$: the sum over
    distinct $n$-tuples becomes $\sum_\sigma \mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)
    a_{1, \sigma(1)} \cdots a_{n, \sigma(n)}$ times $e_1 \wedge \cdots
    \wedge e_n$.

!!! proposition "Proposition (Basis of $\Lambda^d(V)$ from a basis of $V$)"
    <a id="prop-ml-5-2"></a>
    Let $(e_1, \ldots, e_n)$ be a basis of a free $K$-module $V$ and
    $d \in \{1, \ldots, n\}$. The family
    $$\{e_{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge e_{i_d} : 1 \leq i_1 < \cdots < i_d
    \leq n\}$$
    is a basis of $\Lambda^d(V)$ over $K$. In particular
    $\dim_K \Lambda^d(V) = \binom{n}{d}$.

??? proof "Proof (Basis from wedge products)"
    (1) Suppose $\sum_{i=1}^{n} \lambda_i e_i = 0$ with some $\lambda_k
    \neq 0$. Then $0 = (\sum \lambda_i e_i) \wedge e_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge
    e_n = \lambda_1 e_1 \wedge e_2 \wedge \cdots \wedge e_n$ (other
    terms vanish by $e_i \wedge e_i = 0$). So $\lambda_1 e_1 \wedge
    \cdots \wedge e_n = 0$, contradicting the hypothesis.
    (2) Write each generator $x_i = \sum_j a_{i, j} e_j$. By the lemma,
    $x_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge x_d$ is a $K$-linear combination of
    $e_{j_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge e_{j_d}$ with distinct $j_1, \ldots,
    j_d$. By the anti-commutativity each such term equals
    $\pm e_{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge e_{i_d}$ for some
    $i_1 < \cdots < i_d$.
    (3) Already $(e_{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge e_{i_d})$ spans by (2).
    For linear independence, suppose
    $\alpha = \sum_{i_1 < \cdots < i_d} \lambda_{i_1, \ldots, i_d}
    e_{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge e_{i_d} = 0$. Apply
    $e^\vee_{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge e^\vee_{i_d}$ (where $(e^\vee_i)$
    is the dual basis) to both sides. All terms except the one with
    $(i_1, \ldots, i_d)$ vanish (the wedge of dual basis vectors applied
    to a mixed wedge gives $0$ by the same anti-commutativity), giving
    $\lambda_{i_1, \ldots, i_d} = 0$.

!!! corollary "Corollary (Adjunction formula)"
    <a id="cor-ml-5-3"></a>
    For a free module $V$ of rank $n$ and $d \in \{1, \ldots, n\}$, every
    $K$-linear map $F : \Lambda^{n-d}(V) \to \Lambda^n(V)$ is of the form
    $F(\beta) = \alpha \wedge \beta$ for a unique $\alpha \in
    \Lambda^d(V)$.

??? proof "Proof (Adjunction formula)"
    Let $\eta = e_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge e_n$ for a basis $(e_i)$ of $V$
    and $(e_i^\vee)$ its dual. For any $\beta \in \Lambda^{n-d}(V)$,
    $F(\beta)$ is a multiple of $\eta$ (since $\dim \Lambda^n(V) = 1$),
    say $F(\beta) = f(\beta) \eta$ for a $K$-linear form $f$ on
    $\Lambda^{n-d}(V)$. Dualise: $f$ is a linear form on
    $\Lambda^{n-d}(V)$, so it corresponds to an element of
    $\Lambda^{n-d}(V)^\vee \cong \Lambda^d(V)$ by the universal
    property of the exterior power (the linear-form side is the
    $d$-th exterior power of $V^\vee$, naturally identified with
    $\Lambda^d(V)$). Call this element $\alpha \in \Lambda^d(V)$.
    By the explicit formula
    $\langle e^\vee_{j_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge e^\vee_{j_d}, e_{i_1}
    \wedge \cdots \wedge e_{i_{n-d}} \rangle = \delta_{J, \{1, \ldots,
    n\} \setminus I}$ (where $J = \{j_1, \ldots, j_d\}$,
    $I = \{i_1, \ldots, i_{n-d}\}$, both ordered), one verifies
    $F(\beta) = \alpha \wedge \beta$ by checking on basis elements
    $\beta = \beta_I = e_{i_1} \wedge \cdots \wedge e_{i_{n-d}}$.
    Uniqueness: if $\alpha, \alpha'$ both work, then $(\alpha - \alpha')
    \wedge \beta = 0$ for all $\beta$, in particular for
    $\beta = \beta_I$ with $|I| = n - d$ spanning $\Lambda^{n-d}(V)$.
    By linear independence in Thm 4.5.3 (3), $\alpha = \alpha'$.

## Determinant

!!! definition "Definition (Determinant of an endomorphism)"
    <a id="def-ml-6-1"></a>
    Let $V$ be a free $K$-module of rank $n$ and $\varphi : V \to V$ a
    $K$-linear endomorphism. The **determinant** of $\varphi$ is the
    unique element $\det(\varphi) \in K$ such that

    $$
    \varphi(x_1) \wedge \cdots \wedge \varphi(x_n) = \det(\varphi)\,
    x_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge x_n
    $$

    for all $(x_1, \ldots, x_n) \in V^n$.

!!! proposition "Proposition (Multiplicativity)"
    <a id="prop-ml-6-2"></a>
    For $\varphi, \psi \in \mathrm{End}_K(V)$,
    $\det(\psi \circ \varphi) = \det(\psi) \det(\varphi)$ and
    $\det(\mathrm{Id}_V) = 1$.

??? proof "Proof (Characterisation of invertibility)"
    (**⇒**) If $\varphi$ is invertible, $\varphi^{-1}$ is also
    $K$-linear. By multiplicativity $\det(\varphi) \det(\varphi^{-1}) =
    \det(\mathrm{Id}_V) = 1$, so $\det \varphi$ is invertible with
    inverse $\det(\varphi^{-1})$.
    (**⇐**) If $\det \varphi$ is invertible, let $(e_i)$ be a basis of
    $V$ and set $x_i = \varphi(e_i)$. By Lemma 4.5.1,
    $x_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge x_n = \det(\varphi)\, e_1 \wedge \cdots
    \wedge e_n \neq 0$, so $(x_i)$ is linearly independent (Thm 4-5-3).
    Define the alternating $n$-linear form
    $g(y_1, \ldots, y_n) = \det(\varphi)^{-1} \cdot
    (e_1^\vee \wedge \cdots \wedge e_n^\vee)(\varphi^{-1}(y_1) \wedge
    \cdots \wedge \varphi^{-1}(y_n))$. The map
    $f(y_0, y_1, \ldots, y_n) := \sum_{i=0}^{n} (-1)^i g(y_0, \ldots,
    \widehat{y_i}, \ldots, y_n) y_i$ is alternating in the $y_i$ (the
    usual determinant-style argument), so it factors through
    $y_0 \wedge y_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge y_n \in \Lambda^{n+1}(V) = 0$.
    Hence $f \equiv 0$ as a function. Apply with
    $(y_1, \ldots, y_n) = (\varphi(e_1), \ldots, \varphi(e_n))$: the
    coefficient of $y_0$ in the expansion is the identity, so
    $y_0 = -\sum_{i=1}^{n} g(y_0, x_1, \ldots, \widehat{x_i}, \ldots, x_n)
    x_i$ is a $K$-linear combination of $x_1, \ldots, x_n$. So
    $(x_i)$ is a basis of $V$, i.e. $\varphi$ is invertible.

!!! proposition "Proposition (Leibniz formula)"
    <a id="prop-ml-6-4"></a>
    For the matrix $A = (a_{i, j}) \in M_n(K)$,

    $$
    \det(A) = \sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\, a_{1, \sigma(1)}
    \cdots a_{n, \sigma(n)}.
    $$

??? proof "Proof (Leibniz formula)"
    Let $(e_1, \ldots, e_n)$ be the canonical basis of $K^n$. For
    each $i$, $A e_i = a_{i, 1} e_1 + \cdots + a_{i, n} e_n$. By
    Lemma 4.5.1,
    $A e_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge A e_n
    = \left( \sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)
    a_{1, \sigma(1)} \cdots a_{n, \sigma(n)} \right)
    e_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge e_n$.
    By definition of the determinant,
    $A e_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge A e_n = \det(A)\, e_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge
    e_n$. Since $\{e_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge e_n\}$ is a basis of
    $\Lambda^n(K^n) \cong K$ (Prop 4.5.3 (3)), the coefficient must
    equal $\det(A)$.

!!! proposition "Proposition (Determinant of the dual endomorphism)"
    <a id="prop-ml-6-5"></a>
    For a free $K$-module $V$ of finite rank, $\det(\varphi^\vee) =
    \det(\varphi)$.

??? proof "Proof (Determinant of the dual endomorphism)"
    Let $(e_i)$ be a basis of $V$ and $(e_i^\vee)$ its dual basis. Then
    $(e_1^\vee \circ \varphi) \wedge \cdots \wedge (e_n^\vee \circ
    \varphi)$ is an element of $\Lambda^n(V^\vee)$, which is
    one-dimensional. Evaluating on $e_1 \wedge \cdots \wedge e_n$ gives
    $(e_1^\vee \wedge \cdots \wedge e_n^\vee)(\varphi(e_1) \wedge \cdots
    \wedge \varphi(e_n)) = \det(\varphi)$ (apply each
    $e_i^\vee$ to the corresponding $\varphi(e_j)$ and use the
    determinant form of the wedge). So
    $(e_1^\vee \circ \varphi) \wedge \cdots \wedge (e_n^\vee \circ
    \varphi) = \det(\varphi)\, e_1^\vee \wedge \cdots \wedge e_n^\vee$,
    which by definition gives $\det(\varphi^\vee) = \det(\varphi)$.

!!! proposition "Proposition (Cauchy–Binet for adjugates)"
    <a id="prop-ml-6-6"></a>
    The **adjugate** $\mathrm{adj}(A)$ of a square matrix $A$ satisfies
    $A\, \mathrm{adj}(A) = \mathrm{adj}(A)\, A = \det(A) \mathrm{Id}$.
    In particular, when $\det A$ is invertible, $A^{-1} = \det(A)^{-1}
    \mathrm{adj}(A)$.

??? proof "Proof (Cauchy–Binet for adjugates)"
    By Cramer's rule, the $(i, j)$ entry of $A \cdot \mathrm{adj}(A)$
    is $\sum_k a_{i, k} C_{j, k}$ where $C_{j, k} = (-1)^{j+k}
    M_{j, k}$ is the $(j, k)$-cofactor. By Laplace expansion along
    row $j$, this is exactly the determinant when $i = j$ and $0$
    when $i \neq j$ (the matrix obtained by replacing row $j$ with row
    $i \neq j$ has two equal rows). So
    $(A\, \mathrm{adj}(A))_{i, j} = \delta_{i, j} \det A$.
    The computation for $\mathrm{adj}(A) A$ is analogous. When $\det
    A$ is invertible, multiply $A\, \mathrm{adj}(A) = \det(A)
    \mathrm{Id}$ on the left by $A^{-1}$ to obtain
    $A^{-1} = \det(A)^{-1} \mathrm{adj}(A)$.
